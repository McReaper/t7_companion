## Draw a plan's walls with one `walls` op: its lines as drawn, its doors as marked

A plan with walls at angles, curves or rooms that aren't rectangles loses its shape as a `layout` of rectangles, and gains a wall between two rectangles of one open space (verified on a real map). Give a `walls` op the plan's wall centrelines instead, each `line` as the plan draws it, running on through its doors (`closed` for a kiosk's four walls), and the plan's `doors` as points. The op puts every corner on the `grid` (8), runs a line within 16 units of an axis exactly on it (a trace's error), lines the two sides of a gap up, and lands an end within 12 of another wall on it, an axis wall's end sliding along its own axis so it stays straight; walls the plan draws slanted keep their angle between grid corners. Where walls meet nothing overlaps: a corner is mitred, a wall ending on another's side stops on its face, walls crossing split the second, three walls at a point mitre the two turning widest and cut the third by both their faces.

- **Doors come from the plan's own marks** — a gap between jambs, a swing arc — never from gaps a trace left: those are a trace's errors, and a door there is a wall missing. Each door is cut in the wall nearest its point, its edges on the grid: `width` 72 clear for the AI (two abreast), `seen` 56 for the eye and the players (nonColliding jambs, each doubled by a `clip_player` brush, so only players stop at 56), a lintel from `height` 96 to the top, a 2-unit `weaponClip detail` trim: zm_giant's doorways are an opening with a lintel and a trim (verified in the install). A wall too short for its door is an error naming the door.
- **An exit to the outside stays a wall**: past it there is no floor.
- **Zones, floors and ceilings are axis boxes** tiling the building, none overlapping another (two slabs over one spot z-fight); their parts outside the walls are never seen. The answer's `part`s name each piece (`wall`, `door 3 lintel`, `door 3 jamb`) for the ops after it.

## Block out a storey with one `layout` op: rooms on a plan, one wall where two meet

Draw the storey as a plan before any box: each room a named rectangle from wall centre to wall centre, rooms meeting along their edges (a `hall` from `[0, 0]` to `[512, 256]`, a `kitchen` from `[512, 0]` beside it), sent as one `t7kb:map_edit` `layout` op. A wall stands centred on every edge, 8 thick (so the hall is 504 by 248 inside), one wall where two rooms meet, every corner filled; each side of a wall takes the `material` of the room it looks into, `exterior` outside and in an outdoor room. Each room gets its floor slab under `z`, its ceiling `height` above (112 by default; none when `outdoor`, a courtyard) and a `reflection_probe`. An opening is a door `to` the next room, or on a `side` of its room, 72 wide by default: two characters pass abreast, where 56 (Treyarch's door) jams a group of zombies (t7kb: 64 to 69 for two, plus leeway for the AI's collision); a `sill` makes it a window (56 × 64 then, the scale standard); `at` is its middle's world x or y, and left out it centres the opening on the shared wall. An opening stays within one stretch of wall, between the walls that meet it: the refusal lists the stretches. The answer's `part` names each piece (`hall floor`, `hall|kitchen wall`) for the `texture` ops after it. A layout is one storey: another over it would double the slab between them, so build upper floors and stairs with boxes. A plan with walls off the axes, curves or rooms that aren't rectangles is a `walls` op's; a floor off the axes is a `shape` op: a solid on any `outline`, concave too, cut into convex brushes. Then detail it — trims, frames, props — on the layout's pieces.


## Cut doors by building around the hole, not with CSG Subtract

**`Selection/CSG/CSG Subtract` carves the selected brush out of every brush it touches, volumes included**, with no warning: a door cutter through a wall of the template's start room also split the start zone's `info_volume`, the `volume_sun`, the `umbra_volume` and the `volume_fpstool` six ways each (verified in a test Radiant). A zone or sun volume in pieces no longer covers what it did. Build a wall with a door as boxes around the hole instead — the wall's two sides and the lintel above, each a box of the same batch: a `walls` op does it for the doors it's given, a `layout` op for its `openings`, each caulking the faces its pieces hide of each other. Done by mistake, one Ctrl+Z (`t7kb:map_command` with `Edit/Undo`) takes the whole subtract back.
